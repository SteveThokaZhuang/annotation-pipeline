Judge the start, end and function of each intervention on the common time axis. Use the other speaker's specific clause at each onset, the next clause by the same speaker, and the recipient's reaction.

1. Early launch. linked_launch means a short first word may introduce the next same-speaker candidate. Read them together. If that whole contribution tries to take the floor while an unfinished utterance is underway, KEEP at the first audible word and MERGE the following clause. Do not wait for the first substantial word. A false start with no coherent follow-through is not an interruption. If the candidate only completes the speaker's own unfinished sentence, require a distinct renewed attempt before KEEP.
2. Opposite direction. After a competing speaker enters, explicitly check the original speaker's next substantive entry. If it begins while the intervening clause is still underway and asserts a competing point or action, it is a new reverse interruption, even if related to the original topic. Answering after a complete question, completing a shared utterance, or briefly agreeing does not reclaim the floor.
3. Feedback inside an intervention. Decide outer and inner events independently. A substantive entry can be an interruption when it displaces an unfinished clause, including when it repeats part of that clause before redirecting it. A short acknowledgment in the opposite direction during the resulting contribution is its own BACKCHANNEL. Merge the interrupter's continuation across that feedback when it completes the same contribution. Do not let an audio vote of NEITHER for a short launch override a clearly linked substantive clause.
4. Separate feedback bursts. Independently timed listener responses count separately when the other person has kept speaking between them. MERGE only words within the same uninterrupted acknowledgment or repetitions delivered as one burst. An answer at the end of a completed question remains a normal response even if its first sound overlaps the last word.
